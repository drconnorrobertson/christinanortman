Big Four CPA Tax Advisory

From PwC to Private Practice: Institutional Rigor, Personal Attention

The Big Four accounting firms train their professionals to handle the most complex tax situations in the world. Multi-national corporations, Fortune 500 companies, and sophisticated financial structures are the daily work of a Big Four tax professional. That training creates a foundation that few other career paths can match.

Christina Nortman, CPA built her career at PricewaterhouseCoopers (PwC) and CohnReznick before joining AE Tax Advisors as Managing Partner of the Northeast Region. She now applies that institutional-grade methodology to the specific needs of privately held businesses and individual investors.

What Big Four Training Teaches

Big Four firms demand a level of technical precision and analytical rigor that becomes second nature to their professionals. Key competencies developed in a Big Four environment include:

  • Multi-level review processes that catch errors and optimize positions before filing
  • Research methodology for complex tax positions backed by IRC code sections, Treasury regulations, and case law
  • Entity-level analysis across partnerships, S corporations, C corporations, and hybrid structures
  • Multi-state and multi-jurisdictional tax compliance and planning
  • Audit defense preparation that anticipates IRS scrutiny at every step

Why High-Income Professionals Benefit from Big Four-Trained Advisors

Business owners earning $500,000 or more per year operate in a tax bracket where small strategic decisions produce large financial outcomes. A Big Four-trained advisor understands not just how to prepare an accurate return, but how to position income, deductions, and entity structures to minimize total tax liability across all jurisdictions.

Christina Nortman applies this approach to every client engagement at AE Tax Advisors. Her process includes a comprehensive review of current and prior-year positions, entity structure evaluation, proactive multi-year planning, and ongoing advisory to adapt the strategy as circumstances change.

From Institutional Practice to Private Advisory

The transition from Big Four to private advisory is deliberate. Christina Nortman chose to work directly with business owners and investors because that is where strategic tax planning produces the most tangible impact. At a Big Four firm, the client is often a corporation. At AE Tax Advisors, the client is the individual behind the business, and the goal is reducing their personal tax burden while growing their net worth.

This shift in focus means deeper relationships, more personalized strategies, and a level of attention that a Big Four firm cannot provide to a mid-market client.
