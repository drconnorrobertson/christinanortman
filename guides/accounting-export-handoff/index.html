Prepare an Accounting Export Handoff Before a Consultation

An accounting export is easier to discuss when the recipient knows which business it describes, the period it covers, the settings used and what remains unfinished. A file named financials may contain a useful report, but the name does not explain whether it is a draft, which entity it concerns or whether later transactions have changed the source. A clear handoff supplies that context without pretending the export has already been reconciled, reviewed or accepted for a particular professional purpose.

This independent preparation guide uses fictional records to show how to describe a handoff. It does not determine tax treatment, prescribe an accounting basis, replace source records or establish that a professional engagement exists. No actual client data is included. Ask the intended recipient what they need and use their approved delivery process. Do not send credentials, one-time codes or a complete collection of sensitive documents simply because a general online checklist mentions accounting records.

Ask what the recipient needs before exporting everything

Start with the purpose of the conversation. A preliminary consultation, a bookkeeping handoff and a specific professional information request may call for different material. Tell the recipient what you are trying to discuss and ask which reports, periods and supporting information are appropriate. A general website article cannot choose that package for an unidentified business. Exporting every available report can make a handoff harder to interpret and expose information that was not needed for the initial question.

Identify the intended recipient and delivery channel through the actual relationship. A public contact form may be suitable for arranging a conversation without being an approved place to send detailed financial records. If you have not received a delivery instruction, ask first. Do not infer that an email address found in an old message is still the right destination for a new request. Keep the purpose and delivery question separate from the content of the reports until you understand what is appropriate to share.

Record the request in practical terms: who asked, what they requested and which question the package is meant to support. Avoid turning a broad inquiry into an invented professional requirement. If the recipient asks for a particular report, preserve the wording and clarify any ambiguity before selecting a similar report with a different meaning. This preparation can reduce avoidable confusion while leaving the professional responsible for deciding whether the material supplied is sufficient for the actual work.

Identify the entity and source clearly

State which business or accounting file the export concerns using the appropriate identifier for the recipient. A person can manage several businesses with similar names, and a report header may use an abbreviation unfamiliar to someone outside the organization. Confirm the actual source company file before exporting. Do not combine entities into one package without making the boundaries clear or without understanding what the recipient requested. A collection of numbers becomes more difficult to assess when the reader cannot tell which business owns the underlying records.

Name the accounting system or source in a useful, factual way. This does not mean sending login details. It means explaining where the report came from and whether it was generated directly or assembled from another export. If a spreadsheet includes manual edits after export, describe that fact rather than presenting it as an untouched system report. Keep the original available through the authorized process so the recipient can distinguish the source data from a working interpretation or reconciliation note.

If you are unsure which company file is authoritative, make that the first question to resolve. Do not choose whichever file has the most recent-looking balance or the tidiest name. A test company, archived copy or incomplete migration can resemble a current file. Ask the responsible person to identify the actual source and record the answer appropriately. The handoff should expose uncertainty about provenance rather than conceal it beneath a polished report cover.

Describe the period and report settings

Record the report's stated start and end dates, or its as-of date where applicable. Those are different report concepts. A period report describes activity over an interval, while an as-of report concerns a point in time. Do not force both into a single date field that obscures the distinction. Retain the report header and settings as available so the recipient can understand what was requested from the system, rather than relying only on a filename such as quarter-end or annual.

State the basis or other relevant settings shown by the actual report without treating that setting as a professional recommendation. This guide does not decide whether a business should use cash or accrual accounting or which presentation is appropriate for a tax or financial-reporting purpose. If you do not understand a setting, preserve it accurately and ask. Do not switch it merely to make the totals resemble another report without discussing the difference and retaining a clear record of what changed.

Describe filters that materially limit the report, such as selected accounts or locations, where they are relevant to the request. A report can have the correct date range and still exclude information the reader expects. If the recipient asked for an unfiltered view, confirm what that means in the actual system and request. Avoid assuming that default settings are comprehensive. A useful handoff says which settings were used and leaves interpretation of their suitability to the appropriate review.

Keep export time separate from report time

The period a report covers is not the same as the time it was generated. A report covering an earlier month can change if later entries, corrections or other authorized work affect that period. Record when the export was created and whether you know of relevant later changes. Do not label a report final solely because its period has ended. An ended calendar interval does not establish that the books were closed, reconciled or reviewed.

Keep the selected file version clear. If a replacement export is sent, identify what it replaces and why, without silently reusing a filename that leaves the recipient unsure whether two different files have the same contents. The organization's appropriate version process may already address this; follow it rather than inventing a competing archive. The practical goal is for the recipient to know which supplied file you mean and what changed in the handoff, not to collect every possible export indefinitely.

Avoid retroactively rewriting the explanation of a report after discovering a problem. If you first described an export as unfiltered and later learn a location filter was active, correct that description explicitly. The correction is useful information. It does not require claiming that the accounting system failed or that the professional should infer which figures are affected. Explain the known setting issue, provide the appropriate replacement if requested and preserve enough context for the recipient to follow the change.

Distinguish source records from summaries

A report summarizes information; it is not necessarily the underlying documentation for each transaction. The IRS describes business transaction records and supporting documents as connected parts of recordkeeping. That is useful context for this handoff, but the agency does not prescribe the fictional consultation package presented here. Ask the recipient which supporting material is relevant to the actual question. Do not assume that a PDF summary replaces the need to retain appropriate original records under applicable requirements.

Describe any supporting records included and the reason they are included. If a bank statement is requested for a particular account and period, identify that scope through the approved process. Do not substitute an unrelated screenshot because it has a similar ending balance. Likewise, do not treat a bank feed connection as proof that every item has been reviewed or categorized. State what you know about the material supplied without extending that statement into a conclusion about the completeness of the books.

Keep sensitive information within the actual delivery arrangements. A worksheet can name a fictional account reference without containing real bank details, employee identifiers or customer information. The example accompanying this guide deliberately avoids those records. In a real handoff, ask what is needed and how it should be delivered. Do not obscure information required for the actual task or alter a source record without direction; equally, do not add unnecessary personal data to a working summary for convenience.

Worked example: a fictional report package

The fictional Maple Services owner wants to ask about a difference between two exported reports. The preparation worksheet lists package example H-301, a period report for July 1 through September 30 and an export creation date of October 26. It records the basis displayed by the fictional report as cash and notes that the report has not been professionally reviewed. These entries are invented context for the communication example, not recommendations about the basis or period a real business should choose.

A second row, H-302, identifies a fictional balance-sheet report as of September 30. It uses the same export date but retains the as-of distinction. A third row records an unresolved question about a location filter. The preparer does not force the two report totals to match or enter an adjusting transaction to remove the difference. Instead, the question asks whether the selected reports and settings are appropriate for the discussion the recipient has been asked to have.

The worksheet contains no financial totals, account numbers, names of real clients or credentials. It demonstrates the context fields that can make an export easier to identify. A real recipient may request different information or a different format. The useful conclusion is not that the fictional package is complete; it is that the handoff makes the report type, date meaning, export time and unresolved setting visible before anyone interprets the numbers or makes a professional decision.

Label the state of the work accurately

Use descriptions you can support. “Exported from the identified company file on October 26” says something different from “reconciled through September.” If you do not know whether reconciliation was completed, say that the status is unknown or ask the responsible person. Do not use a green checkmark to mean several incompatible things at once. A recipient should be able to distinguish a file that opens from a report that someone has reviewed for a particular purpose.

Keep client preparation separate from professional assurance. Supplying records does not establish that a CPA reviewed them, accepted them as complete or issued an opinion. Do not add a professional's name to a worksheet as reviewer unless that person actually performed and authorized the stated review. This website guide is independent editorial content, not evidence that a named professional examined the fictional example or the reader's real books. The actual relationship and work determine what can properly be said.

When several people contribute, identify who can answer which factual questions. An owner may confirm the business identity, a bookkeeper may describe report settings and another person may control the authorized source documents. This does not create new responsibilities or permissions; it helps route questions within the real process. If the handoff relies on someone who has not confirmed a fact, mark it pending rather than filling in their approval because the rest of the package looks ready.

Use a clear cover note

Write the cover note around the recipient's actual request. State the purpose, identify the files supplied and describe any material limitation or open question. Avoid a long narrative that buries the period or entity under unrelated business history. A short note can direct the reader to the relevant context worksheet and preserve the report headers. If a detail is uncertain, say what is uncertain and who may be able to clarify it rather than disguising the uncertainty with confident accounting terminology.

For the fictional example, the note might say: “I am preparing for a discussion about the two report views. H-301 is a July–September period export; H-302 is an as-of September 30 report. Both were generated October 26. The location-filter question remains unresolved, and I have not represented either report as reconciled or professionally reviewed.” This is an illustration of accurate scope, not a model email that should be sent with invented facts or confidential material through an unapproved channel.

Ask for confirmation appropriate to the handoff. Acknowledgement that files were received is different from confirmation that they are complete or suitable. If the recipient requests another export, connect the replacement to the original request so the package does not become a disconnected sequence of attachments. Do not interpret silence as acceptance. The actual consultation process should determine the next step, including whether more information is needed before a productive conversation can occur.

Open the actual exported file before describing it

Check that the file you intend to supply opens and displays the report header you described. A download folder can contain an earlier export with the same report name, while a spreadsheet can open on a different tab from the one you meant to discuss. Identify the relevant sheet where needed and preserve the report context. Opening a file is a modest technical check; it does not establish that the accounting information is correct or that the selected period is appropriate.

In the fictional handoff, a reviewer notices that H-302's cover note says September 30 while the exported report header says August 31. The team flags the mismatch and asks for the intended report rather than changing the header in a document editor. Once the appropriate export is identified, the handoff note can be corrected. Keeping the report and its description aligned avoids asking the recipient to infer which date the preparer really intended.

Do not confuse a consultation handoff with an examination request

The IRS publishes separate questions and answers about electronic accounting records in examinations. That material concerns its stated examination context; it is not a generic instruction to send a full accounting backup whenever you arrange a consultation. Keep those situations distinct. If you receive a specific official or professional request, follow the appropriate process for that request and seek relevant assistance. Do not use this article to select a response to an examination, determine record-retention obligations or decide which data may be withheld.

Similarly, a client's convenience export is not automatically the format needed for every engagement. A spreadsheet, PDF report and native accounting file provide different kinds of information. Ask which format is needed for the actual task and understand the delivery arrangements. Do not convert or strip a file in a way that removes required detail merely because a general checklist favors a smaller attachment. Preserve the source through the authorized process while clarifying the appropriate working representation.

A useful handoff ends with an accurate account of what was supplied and what remains open. It does not manufacture reconciliation, completeness or a tax conclusion. Keep the entity, date meaning, settings and export context connected to the actual files, then let the recipient ask the next relevant question. That preparation makes the conversation easier to follow while respecting the boundaries between organizing records, assessing accounting information and performing a specific professional service.

Frequently asked questions

Should I send every report before a first consultation?

Ask the intended recipient what is appropriate for the actual purpose and how to deliver it. A preliminary conversation may need a different scope from an established bookkeeping or tax engagement. Do not send sensitive records through a public contact form simply because it accepts attachments. Identify the question, requested period and approved channel first. This guide cannot choose a complete records package for an unidentified business or establish that a professional relationship exists.

Is an export date the same as the period covered?

No. A report may cover an earlier interval or as-of date while being generated later. Record both meanings clearly. Later entries or corrections can affect a report concerning a prior period, so an ended calendar month does not establish that the export is final or the books are closed. Describe the selected file and known changes without inventing a review or reconciliation status. Ask the responsible person when the state of the work is uncertain.

Should I change the basis to match another report?

Do not switch a setting merely to make totals look similar. Preserve the actual settings, identify the difference and ask which report is appropriate for the question. This guide does not recommend an accounting basis or determine tax or financial-reporting treatment. A report setting is context for the recipient, not a conclusion about what the business should use. If a replacement is requested, make clear what it replaces and which settings changed.

Does sending a bank statement prove the books are reconciled?

No. Supplying a source document and completing a reconciliation are different actions. Describe which records are included and avoid claiming a review you cannot support. The recipient may need particular documents or further context for the actual task. Do not substitute a similar-looking screenshot or an account connection for a requested record without clarification. Keep the statement's period and account scope identifiable through the approved process without adding unnecessary sensitive information to a public worksheet.

Can I call the package CPA-reviewed after I upload it?

No. Uploading or sending records does not establish that a professional reviewed them, accepted them as complete or provided assurance. State only the work actually performed and authorized. An acknowledgement of receipt is a different event from a substantive review. This independent guide contains fictional examples and does not assert named professional review. Use the actual engagement and communication process to understand what the recipient has done and what remains to be requested or assessed.

What should I do if I discover a wrong filter after sending?

Correct the description explicitly through the actual handoff channel and explain which file or setting is affected. Ask whether a replacement is needed, and identify what it replaces if one is provided. Do not silently reuse the same filename or alter source transactions to make the reports appear consistent. Keep the relevant context available so the recipient can follow the change. The useful response is accurate information about the error, not an unsupported conclusion about the resulting accounting treatment.
